Both detectors (`ardoco-id`, `artemis-id`) report two finding types with the same evidence shape (`Mapping.java`):

| Type | Claim | Involved | Evidence used in the sheet |
|---|---|---|---|
| `undocumented-model-element` | The model contains a component that the documentation does not mention. | the `ModelElement` | `elementName`, `elementType` |
| `missing-model-element` | A sentence names architecture elements that the model does not contain. | the sentence `TextUnit` (with `excerpt`) | `sentence` (1-based), `words[].word` |

## Goal

Two people label a stratified random sample of findings without knowing the detector or the confidence. The study reports precision per stratum with a 95 % CI, pooled precision per detector and type, and the inter-rater agreement (Cohen's κ with a 95 % CI).

## Scope

1. **Rater guidelines** `experiments/labeling/GUIDELINES.md`: the label scheme, the decision rules per finding type, examples, and the procedure.
2. **`sandman eval label-sample`** (`sandman_cli.evaluation.labeling`):
   - Input: one or more files from `sandman export inconsistencies --project P --format json > P.json`.
   - Population: findings of the detectors `ardoco-id` and `artemis-id` (`--detector` to change), status not `RESOLVED`.
   - Stratum: detector × finding type × project. Seeded sample without replacement per stratum (`--per-stratum`, `--seed`). The sample of a stratum does not depend on the other strata.
   - Item: the rater-visible content of a finding. Findings with identical content (for example, the same component reported by both detectors) form one item. The label then counts for each of these findings.
   - Output in `--out DIR`: `rater-a.csv` and `rater-b.csv` (same items, each sheet in its own shuffled order, opaque ids `i0001`, ...) and `key.json` (strata sizes, item-to-finding map with detector, stratum and confidence, SHA-256 of each export file).
   - Sheet columns: `item, project, type, artifact, sentence_no, sentence, element, element_kind, terms, label, note`. The sheet has no detector, detector version, confidence, severity, title, description or triage status.
3. **`sandman eval label-score --key K --a A --b B [--adjudicated F] [--disagreements OUT]`**:
   - Checks that both sheets label every item with `true`, `false` or `unclear`.
   - Agreement: observed agreement and Cohen's κ (three categories) with a 95 % percentile bootstrap CI over items, for all items and per finding type.
   - Disagreements: the list in the report, and with `--disagreements` an adjudication sheet (item view, both labels and notes, empty `label` and `note`).
   - Final label: the agreed label, else the label of the filled adjudication sheet (`--adjudicated`). Items without a final label are `unresolved` and are not in the precision.
   - Per stratum: population, sample size, counts per label, precision = true / (true + false), 95 % Wilson CI.
   - Pooled per detector × type and per detector: stratified estimate with weights N_h / ΣN_h, normal CI with finite population correction.
4. **Statistics** in `sandman_cli.evaluation.stats`: `cohens_kappa`, `kappa_ci`, `wilson`, `stratified_proportion` (standard library only).

Out: the study itself (human raters); a rating UI; changes to the query-service export; recall (the ArDoCo gold standard covers recall, §3.3 table); per-word labels of `missing-model-element` (T3.1 decision 1).

## Procedure (when the study runs)

1. Run the detectors on the benchmark projects. Record the experiment manifest (`scripts/experiment_manifest.py`, PROTOCOL §8).
2. Export the findings per project: `sandman export inconsistencies --project P --format json > exports/P.json`.
3. Create the sheets: `sandman eval label-sample --export exports/mediastore.json ... --out experiments/labeling/<study>`.
4. Give `rater-a.csv` to rater A and `rater-b.csv` to rater B, each with `GUIDELINES.md` and the project documents. **Caution:** Do not give `key.json` to a rater.
5. Run a calibration round on 10 findings that are not in the sample (guideline §6). Change the guidelines only before step 3, or log the change as a deviation.
6. The raters label independently. They do not talk about items until both sheets are complete.
7. Score: `sandman eval label-score --key key.json --a rater-a.csv --b rater-b.csv --disagreements adjudication.csv`. Record κ before adjudication.
8. Adjudicate (decision 7), then score again with `--adjudicated adjudication.csv`. Archive the sheets, the key and both reports with the manifest.

## Acceptance criteria

- κ equals hand-computed values: the 2 × 2 table 20/5/10/15 gives κ = 0.4; chance-level agreement gives κ = 0; identical labels give κ = 1.
- Wilson CI equals reference values (5 of 10 gives [0.2366, 0.7634]; 0 of 10 gives [0, 0.2775]).
- The stratified estimate of a census has a CI of width 0.
- `label-sample` is deterministic for a seed, keeps the strata independent, excludes `RESOLVED` and other detectors, merges identical items, and the sheets contain no detector name, confidence, severity, status or title.
- `label-score` rejects unknown, missing and invalid labels, lists the disagreements, writes the adjudication sheet, and uses adjudicated labels in the precision.
- `ruff`, `mypy --strict` and `pytest tools/cli` pass.

## Decisions

1. **Strata.** [Detector × finding type × project.] With 2 detectors, 2 types and 5 benchmark projects there are at most 20 strata. Pro: precision per type is the §3.3 metric; per project shows project effects. Con: many small strata. A stratum with fewer findings than the sample size is a census.
2. **Sample size.** [30 findings per stratum, census below 30.] Power note: with n = 30 the 95 % Wilson half-width is at most 0.17 (p = 0.5) and 0.14 at p = 0.8. Pooled over 5 projects (n ≈ 150 per detector × type) the half-width is about 0.08 (p = 0.5) and 0.06 (p = 0.8). For κ with n ≈ 300 items, observed agreement 0.85 and chance agreement 0.5, the large-sample SE is about 0.04, so the 95 % CI half-width is about 0.08. This is enough to tell "moderate" (0.4–0.6) from "substantial" (0.6–0.8) agreement. Cost: at most 600 findings, fewer items after merging; at about 1 minute per item this is up to 10 hours per rater. If the budget is smaller, use 20 per stratum (half-width ≤ 0.21 per stratum, ≈ 0.10 pooled).
3. **Population.** [All findings of the two detectors with status other than `RESOLVED` at export time.] `ACCEPTED` and `REJECTED` stay in, because excluding triaged items repeats the selection bias of R-044. `RESOLVED` items can refer to old element versions.
4. **Blinding.** [Raters see project, type, artifact path, sentence number, sentence text, element name and kind, flagged terms. They do not see detector, version, confidence, severity, title, description or triage status. Sheets have opaque ids and a different order per rater.] Residual risk: the flagged terms of `artemis-id` (named entities) can look different from the words of `ardoco-id`. Report this as a threat to validity.
5. **Identical items.** [Merged.] The same component flagged by both detectors is one item. Pro: less work, no hint from seeing the same claim twice, one consistent label. Con: κ counts the item once, precision counts it in both strata.
6. **Label scheme.** [`true` (true inconsistency), `false` (false positive), `unclear` (the rater cannot decide with the documents).] Precision excludes `unclear`; the report lists the count per stratum. A sensitivity value that counts `unclear` as false can be computed from the counts.
7. **Disagreement resolution.** [The two raters discuss each disagreement after both sheets are complete and agree on one label in the adjudication sheet. If they do not agree, a third person (the study lead, who did not rate) decides. κ is reported before adjudication.]
8. **κ confidence interval.** [95 % percentile bootstrap over items, 10 000 resamples, seed 20261017.] Same conventions as PROTOCOL §3. The analytic large-sample SE is not implemented.
9. **Pooled CI.** [Stratified estimator with a normal CI and finite population correction.] The CI is too narrow if a sampled stratum is all `true` or all `false`; the code marks this ceiling. Wilson CIs per stratum are exact enough.
10. **Pre-registration.** [The guidelines and this spec are committed before sampling. Record their commit in the study folder. Log later changes as deviations, as PROTOCOL.md requires.]
